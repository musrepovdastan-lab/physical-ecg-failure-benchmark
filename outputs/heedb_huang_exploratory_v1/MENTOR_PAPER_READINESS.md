# Mentor summary: physical ECG electrode-reversal benchmark

**Proposed paper framing:** a methods/robustness study showing that physically exact limb-cable swaps expose a failure that ordinary missing-lead masks can miss, and that a simple physics-aware augmentation can protect diagnostic ranking without fault detection. Present the Huang detect-and-correct approach as a serious, published operational comparator—not a defeated baseline. The HEEDB Huang result is explicitly **post-hoc exploratory**.

**Problem and data.** A conventional 12-lead ECG has algebraic structure: swapping the RA and LL cables changes five displayed leads, not merely their availability. We test a clean-trained small ResNet on Chapman–Shaoxing (2,050 test recordings) and independent Ningbo (6,981 test recordings) with three matched seeds and T-wave abnormality (TWA), sinus bradycardia and sinus tachycardia outcomes. A later external HEEDB/Emory test uses two disjoint 10,000-patient samples, one ECG each, with 12SL `codes_physician` outcome proxies. All swaps are **electrically exact software simulations**, not verified real miswirings. HEEDB physician-code TWA is clinically related to, but not identical with, the Chapman/Ningbo SNOMED construct.

**Prior approaches and our method.** Five-displayed-lead masking is a conventional robustness comparator; it does not reproduce a cable permutation. Huang et al.'s published LDenseNet detects clean/RA–LA/RA–LL/LA–LL from III, aVR and V6; its predicted class can be inverted before a clean diagnostic model. Our physics-aware approach trains one diagnostic ResNet with exact, label-preserving electrical-reversal augmentation, without a detector at inference. This is an application and evaluation of known circuit algebra with a deployment-relevant baseline—not a claim to have invented lead-reversal physics or surpassed the published detector.

**Results.** In Chapman, the three-seed mean baseline TWA AUPRC is about .653 clean, .328 under RA–LL and .574 for the mean of all 792 matched five-lead masks; the paired mask-minus-RA–LL contrast is +.246 [95% CI .210,.280]. Ningbo replicates the failure: baseline TWA AUPRC about .676 clean, .182 RA–LL and .514 mean mask; contrast +.331 [.311,.350]. On Ningbo, physics-aware training raises RA–LL TWA AUPRC to about .667 and preserves clean ranking; the matched-contrast reduction versus baseline is +.460 [.435,.484]. A prior, frozen Ningbo operational comparison found Huang detect→correct→clean ResNet at .664 RA–LL TWA AUPRC, versus .667 physics-aware; the paired difference was unresolved, **not evidence of equivalence**. The single physics-aware pipeline was 2.97× faster than the complete Huang chain in one separate warm CPU request-to-response implementation benchmark; this is hardware/software-specific.

After correction of two documented HEEDB v1 implementation errors, clean ranking transported. On the **new, patient-disjoint** HEEDB independent cohort, baseline versus physics-aware core-TWA AUPRC is .573/.576 clean and .165/.568 under RA–LL; the protected-minus-baseline RA–LL difference is +.404 [independent protocol's paired 95% CI .379,.426]. The physics-aware RA–LL sensitivity is **.687 versus .697** baseline at their frozen thresholds, while specificity is .891 versus .360: this is a ranking/specificity gain, **not a sensitivity gain**. Secondary HEEDB SB/STach and strict-code results appear in the frozen independent report; they are not silently merged with the TWA primary outcome.

The new **exploratory** HEEDB Huang pass on those same 10,000 patients uses published unchanged detector weights and no true-fault oracle. It reaches RA–LL TWA AUPRC **.581**, AUROC **.907**, sensitivity **.655**, specificity **.912**, versus physics-aware **.568/.890/.687/.891**. Physics-aware minus Huang AUPRC is −.0128 [−.0280,+.0019]: no resolved AUPRC difference and no equivalence claim. Huang has better AUROC and calibration; physics-aware has higher fixed-threshold sensitivity but lower specificity. The detector leaves 97.01% of clean ECGs untouched and correctly identifies 99.19% of simulated RA–LL ECGs; its mistakes measurably perturb some final diagnoses. Neither method earns a clinical-superiority or SOTA claim.

**Novelty and limits.** The defensible contribution is a consequence-matched physical-fault benchmark across datasets, a separately confirmed mitigation under external physician-code proxy labels, and a transparent detector-correction comparison with operating-point and calibration tradeoffs. The strongest limit is that no cohort verifies actual wrong cable placement or clinical utility. Other limits are label mapping ambiguity, acquisition/population shifts, a single diagnostic architecture/fault family, 12SL proxy labels, and post-hoc timing of the HEEDB Huang comparison. An interval crossing zero cannot establish equivalence. Rate-label effects do not prove which morphology features the model uses.

**Submission recommendation.** A bounded methods/robustness workshop or carefully scoped journal paper is supportable now if its central claim is *simulated physical-fault vulnerability and mitigation of ranking*, not clinical diagnosis improvement or superiority over Huang. For that framing, the remaining essential work is manuscript-level verification: independently rerun the hashed aggregate tables/figures and code/protocol provenance, document HEEDB access/publication restrictions, and have a clinical collaborator review the 12SL→TWA/SB/STach proxy mapping and wording. A study of **verified real miswirings with adjudicated labels** is necessary only before advancing a real-world clinical-effectiveness claim; it is not a prerequisite for this explicitly simulation-bounded methods submission. No additional training or broad method search is justified by the present comparison.

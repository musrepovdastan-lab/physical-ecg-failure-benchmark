# Mentor brief — simulated ECG cable-reversal robustness

**Bottom line.** We have a defensible simulation-bounded methods/workshop paper: electrically exact *simulated* limb-cable reversals create a diagnostic failure not exposed by five-lead masking matched on the number of affected displayed leads. Training on the electrical fault family preserves T-wave-abnormality (TWA) ranking in Chapman, Ningbo and an independent HEEDB/Emory physician-code-proxy cohort. We should not frame this as validated clinical benefit, superiority to a published detector, or performance on real wiring errors.

**Problem and approach.** A cable reversal leaves an ECG apparently complete but changes a coherent set of leads; missing-lead masking removes information instead. We tested a clean-trained 12-lead ResNet against matched physics-aware training (50% clean; 50% exact RA-LA/RA-LL/LA-LL simulations), three matched seeds, fixed preprocessing and Ningbo thresholds. A fixed panel of all 792 five-displayed-lead masks served as a consequence-count control. Chapman and Ningbo were the original source/follow-up test sets. HEEDB I0006/Emory supplied a later, new 10,000-patient confirmation disjoint from the earlier corrected-v2 10,000 and 23 readiness patients. Its primary labels were physician-overread 12SL codes, not exact Chapman/Ningbo SNOMED outcomes. No HEEDB model fitting or recalibration occurred.

**Results.** In Chapman, clean-trained TWA AUPRC was 0.653 clean, 0.574 averaged across masks, and 0.328 under exact simulated RA-LL; physics-aware RA-LL was 0.637. Ningbo analogues were 0.676, 0.514 and 0.182; physics-aware RA-LL was 0.667. In independent HEEDB, the TWA proxy prevalence was 0.1456. Under simulated RA-LL, unprotected versus physics-aware AUPRC was 0.165 versus 0.568 (paired difference +0.404, 95% CI 0.379–0.426); clean AUPRC was 0.573 versus 0.576. The RA-LL benefit was ranking and specificity (0.360→0.891), **not sensitivity** at the frozen threshold (0.697→0.687). The unprotected model flagged 64.8% of ECGs, versus 19.3% physics-aware. Calibration worsened relative to clean baseline even for the physics-aware model (HEEDB clean ECE15 0.112 versus 0.077), and no HEEDB calibration was performed. SB/STach and strict-label analyses are supportive but label-dependent; strict TWA had an unfavorable physics-aware sensitivity contrast.

**Published comparator.** Huang et al. (2024) published a lead-misplacement detector, not a downstream diagnostic model. We applied its unchanged detector, let its *prediction* choose correction, then ran the saved clean ResNet on the same HEEDB patients. This comparison was chosen **after** seeing HEEDB outcomes, so it is exploratory. Huang→clean ResNet gave RA-LL TWA AUPRC 0.581 versus 0.568 physics-aware; physics-aware minus Huang -0.013 [-0.028, +0.002]. That is unresolved—not equivalence, not a demonstrated AUPRC disadvantage for Huang, and not SOTA superiority for either method. Huang had better RA-LL AUROC, specificity and calibration; physics-aware had higher sensitivity under its own unchanged threshold. The detector made 299 false corrections on clean and 81 wrong calls on simulated RA-LL out of 10,000 each.

**Novelty and limits.** The contribution is a physically grounded failure mode and an unusually transparent ranking/operating-point comparison with missingness and detect-and-correct controls, not a new universal ECG architecture. All faults were simulated; true acquisition errors, clinical outcomes, broad architecture generality and HEEDB label equivalence remain untested. We independently rechecked saved scores, 1,000-resample intervals, protocol hashes, cohort separation and figure data without rerunning any benchmark. Restricted HEEDB waveforms/predictions stay private under BDSP v5.0 terms.

**Recommendation.** Circulate the [manuscript draft](MANUSCRIPT_DRAFT.md) and aggregate figures for coauthor review, obtain a clinician ruling on the seven precise 12SL mapping questions, and have the institution review BDSP data-use and code-release obligations. Then target a simulation-bounded methods/workshop venue. A stronger clinical or top-tier ML claim would need independently verified real wiring errors with adjudicated labels and a prespecified operational workflow, plus broader model/site testing—not just another post-hoc HEEDB analysis.
